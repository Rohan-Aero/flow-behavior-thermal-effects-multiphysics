## 2. What each difference is made of

### Row 1 — Reynolds number: agrees to 0.005 %

Both use the same viscosity table. The CFD mass flux is G = ṁ/A = 8.662155 g/s ÷ 3.1326 × 10⁻⁴ m²
= 27.651 kg/m²s, against 27.650 analytically. The 0.005 % difference is density: Fluent's
incompressible-ideal-gas law with M = 28.966 kg/kmol implies R = 287.0425 J/kg·K, against
287.058 in Section 2. That is more than 200 × smaller than the 1.3 % table-density error D-021
exists to prevent. Faceting does **not** enter Re: the 48-gon reduces both ṁ and A by the same factor, so
G is unchanged.

### Row 2 — Pressure drop: −0.77 %, but read the decomposition before quoting it

**What the CFD number is.** 438.13 Pa is the difference in area-weighted static gauge pressure
between the inlet face and the outlet face of the computational domain (mass-weighted gives
438.13 Pa too; the section is almost uniform in pressure). It contains every effect that exists
between those two faces and nothing else.

**What the analytical number is.** 441.53 Pa = fully developed friction 263.16 + 1-D
acceleration 149.13 + developing-flow entry increment 29.24 (D-019). That is the CFD-comparable
figure. The 1003.4 Pa "installation" figure adds a sharp-entrance and a sudden-exit loss that
do not exist in this domain and is **never** compared with Fluent.

**Decomposition of the CFD value.** The axial momentum balance on the exported solution is

  (p_in − p_out)·A = F_wall + (M_out − M_in),  M = ∫ ρw² dA

and it closes to −0.197 Pa (−0.045 %), so the split below is a measurement, not a model:

| Term | CFD | Analytical counterpart | Analytical |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wall shear, F_wall/A | **242.87 Pa** | fully developed friction | 263.16 Pa |
| 1-D acceleration, G²(1/ρ_out − 1/ρ_in), CFD bulk densities | **149.31 Pa** | 1-D acceleration | 149.13 Pa |
| Momentum-flux non-uniformity (β_out = 1.058 vs plug inlet β = 1.000) | **46.15 Pa** | part of the entry increment | 29.24 Pa |
| **Total** | **438.13 Pa** | | **441.53 Pa** |

- **Acceleration agrees to 0.12 %.** That is conservation plus the ideal-gas law, and both are
  right.
- **Wall shear is 7.7 % low.** It includes the high-shear entry region, so the fully developed
  deficit is larger: f = 0.02163 at x/D 18–29 against 0.02413 (−10.4 %).
- **The non-uniformity term is 58 % above the analytical entry increment.** The analytical K_dev
  = 0.09 is an isothermal handbook value; here the outlet section also carries a hot, low-density
  wall layer, which raises the momentum-flux coefficient to 1.058, well above the 1.020 of an
  isothermal 1/7-power profile.

**So the −0.77 % agreement is partly compensating:** the CFD wall shear is lower and its
momentum-flux term higher than the analytical model's. The total is a fair like-for-like
comparison; the components are not the same split, and neither agreement should be quoted
without the other.

Against the older requirement PR-05 (412 Pa = friction + acceleration only, written before
D-019 added the entry term), the CFD is +6.3 %, inside PR-05's ±15 %.

### Row 3 — Outlet temperature: fully explained by faceting

The 1-D energy balance with the same c_p table, applied to Section 2's inputs, gives
368.852 K — Section 2 reproduced to three decimals. Applied instead to the heat input and
mass flow **the CFD mesh actually carries** (602.755 W, 8.662155 g/s, both reduced by the
48-gon), it gives **368.996 K**: a shift of **+0.144 K caused by faceting alone**.

The CFD mixing-cup temperature from Fluent's enthalpy field is **368.993 K**, 0.003 K from that
prediction (the 0.02 % face-value re-analysis of ṁ). The mass-weighted static temperature is
lower, 368.932 K, because c_p rises with temperature: the enthalpy average is the like-for-like
counterpart of the energy march, the static-temperature average sits 0.06 K below it.

There is therefore **no physics disagreement** in outlet temperature. PR-02 (±3 %) is met with a
margin of more than 75× on either outlet measure (+0.022 % mass-weighted, +0.038 % mixing-cup).

### Row 4 — Heat-transfer rate: exact, up to geometry

Fluent applies 8000 W/m² to the 48-gon's outer area, 0.0753444 m², against 0.0753982 m² for a
true cylinder. The ratio is 0.999286 — exactly sin(π/48)/(π/48). The CFD heat rate is low by
**−0.0714 %; the area ratio predicts −0.0714 %**. To seven significant figures the boundary
condition is applied correctly and the only difference is the polygonal wall. PR-01 (±0.5 %) is
met.

### Row 5 — Maximum solid temperature: −19 K, and it is the film, not the metal

The maximum solid temperature sits where it must: on the outer surface at the downstream end
(outer-wall facet maximum 562.58 K at the last facet row, centroid z = 596.7 mm — extrapolating
to the corner at z = 600 mm adds < 0.05 K; the hottest cell centre is 562.13 K at r = 19.1 mm). Its analytical counterpart is the outer-wall temperature at the exit, 581.71 K.

The difference splits cleanly:

| | Analytical | CFD | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bulk air at exit | 368.85 K | 368.93 K | +0.08 K (faceting, row 3) |
| Wall-to-bulk rise across the air film | 205.58 K | 186.33 K | **−19.24 K** |
| Rise across the Inconel wall | 7.28 K | 7.31 K | +0.03 K |
| **Outer wall at exit** | **581.71 K** | **562.58 K** | **−19.14 K** (components rounded) |

Essentially the **whole** difference is in the air film. The metal agrees (row 6), the bulk air
agrees (row 3). The film rise is q″ᵢ/h, and **both** factors differ at the outlet end, so the
−19.24 K splits exactly into two measured parts:

| Film-rise component at the outlet end | Analytical | CFD | Effect on the film rise |
|---|---|---|---|
| h (last slab) | 77.83 W/m²K | 83.95 W/m²K (+7.9 %) | **−15.0 K** |
| Bore heat flux q″ᵢ (last slab) | 16 000 W/m² (1-D) | 15 644 W/m² (−2.2 %) | **−4.2 K** |
| **Film rise, q″ᵢ/h** | **205.58 K** | **186.33 K** | **−19.24 K** |

- **−15.0 K — higher h.** The CFD transfers heat from the wall to the air more easily than the
  property-corrected correlation assumed. At the same station, z = 570 mm, the CFD gives
  **h = 83.6 W/m²K** against 77.5 W/m²K analytically (+7.9 %).
- **−4.2 K — less heat reaches the bore at the outlet end.** Near the adiabatic outlet end
  face the Inconel conducts part of the applied heat axially back toward the cooler upstream
  wall, so the last slab's bore flux is 2.2 % below the 1-D 16 000 W/m². This is a conjugate
  end effect the 1-D march excludes by construction (radial conduction only). At z = 570 mm
  the same split is −15.2 K (h) and −2.2 K (flux, −1.2 %).

Why the h differs:

1. **The property-variation correction is the dominant lever.** Section 2 flagged this (F-006):
   the wall-to-bulk ΔT is 206 K against a correlation validity limit of about 60 K, and it
   applied Kays & Crawford's `(T_b/T_w)^n` with n = 0.5. Measured against Gnielinski at the CFD's
   own local conditions, the CFD's fully developed Nusselt number corresponds to
   **n ≈ 0.36** — a weaker property penalty than n = 0.5 and slightly below the literature range
   0.4–0.575. Evaluated at the CFD's own temperatures, the n = 0.5 correlation gives Nu = 51.0;
   the CFD gives 54.2 (+6.1 %); constant-property Gnielinski gives 63.5 and Dittus–Boelter 68.6.
   The CFD sits inside the Section 2 band 44–67 (PR-03 met) and, as Section 2 anticipated, much
   nearer the corrected end than the constant-property end.
2. **The film carries almost all the resistance, so this propagates in full.** Section 2 found
   R_conv/R_total = 0.966. A 7.9 % higher h lowers the wall-to-bulk rise by
   1 − 1/1.079 = 7.3 %, which at 206 K is the 15.0 K in the table above. That is why F-010
   warned that turbulence-model error propagates strongly into solid temperature.
3. **It is not a y⁺ artefact.** The wall is resolved to y⁺ ≤ 0.59 everywhere (§4 of the
   results), so the SST model is integrating to the wall, not using a wall function. The
   difference is the model's own response to strongly varying properties, set against a
   correlation that is itself being used outside its range.

PR-06 (inner wall within 5 % of the corrected march): −3.0 % at z = 570 mm, −3.3 % at the exit —
**met**. EXPECTED_RESULTS band for the maximum solid temperature, 541–591 K: **inside**.

### Row 6 — Through-wall ΔT: +1.0 %

At z = 570 mm (x/D = 28.5, clear of the adiabatic end face) the CFD gives 7.361 K against
the Section 2 headline 7.285 K (+1.04 %). That headline is an exit value: Section 2 evaluated
k = 15.22 W/m·K at the 574.4 K exit inner-wall temperature. At the **same station** as the CFD
value the Section 2 march gives 7.306 K, so the station-matched difference is **+0.74 %**.

The CFD wall at z = 570 mm is about 17 K cooler than the analytical wall, and the VDM curve
gives a lower k there. Using k at the CFD's own local solid temperature and the nominal
8000 W/m² outer flux, the 1-D Fourier law gives 7.412 K, and the CFD is **0.7 % below that** —
the small share of heat that travels axially through the Inconel instead of straight across it
(the bore flux at this station is 15 815 W/m², 1.2 % below 16 000). PR-07 (±10 %) is met.

The same quantity is **not** 1-D everywhere, and the CFD shows exactly where the 1-D model stops
holding (Figures 8 and 12):

- In the **first slab** (z ≈ 3 mm) the bore heat flux is **38 471 W/m², 2.4 × the 1-D value**,
  and the through-wall ΔT is **15.1 K**. The inlet air is coldest and its thermal boundary layer
  thinnest, so h is very high there, and heat conducts axially through the Inconel toward the
  inlet end to supply it.
- Just downstream the bore flux dips to 14 825 W/m², recovering to within 1 % of 16 000 W/m² by
  z ≈ 250 mm.
- At the outlet end it falls to 15 644 W/m² as the adiabatic end face turns some heat axially.

This matters for Section 6: the structural model will see a temperature field with genuine
axial gradients near both ends that the Section 2 closed form does not contain. T-013 already
restricts the FEA-vs-closed-form comparison to mid-span for this reason.

PR-04 is reported as it stands, not re-interpreted: the fully developed friction factor on the
medium mesh is 10.4 % below the Section 2 value against a ±10 % criterion. Part of that is
physics the original criterion did not include — gas heating lowers f, and the standard

points. Whether the rest is turbulence-model behaviour or mesh is exactly what the mesh study
has to answer, which is why it is not declared either way here.
