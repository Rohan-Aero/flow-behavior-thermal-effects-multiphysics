## 2. Set-up on every mesh (identical to 8A)

- **System.** A Linear Buckling system linked to the LC2 static solution of the same mesh, added by the same journal code as in 8A. The pre-solve gate confirms, per mesh:
  - pre-stress environment = `LC2_Axially_Restrained`;
  - no loads or supports of the buckling system itself;
  - six modes; negative multipliers excluded;
  - solver Program Controlled.
- **Solver.** MAPDL perturbation restart (`perturb,buckle,,CURRENT,ALLKEEP`, Block Lanczos, 6 modes). λ multiplies the whole LC2 thermal load and all LC2 constraints are kept.
- **Critical load.** N is the LC2 end reaction of the *same* mesh, 548,936–548,943 N. That is why λ₁ and P_cr behave slightly differently in §5.
- **Solver messages.** 0 errors on every mesh. The warnings are the known ones: shape checking inactive, out-of-core sparse solver, and `*VGET` undefined at midside nodes (F-041) in the static post-snippet.
- **Consistency of the reported multipliers.** The six values in `solve.out`, the snippet table `s8a_load_factors.csv` and Mechanical's `LoadMultiplier` are identical on every mesh (independent verification, `STRUCTURAL_MESH_AUDIT.md` §6).

**Why FR and FC move λ₁ more than P_cr.** Through-wall and circumferential refinement raise the LC2 end reaction N by 5.7 × 10⁻⁶ (FR) and 7.3 × 10⁻⁶ (FC) (§7 of `STRUCTURAL_MESH_COMPARISON.md`). λ₁ falls by about the same amount, while the critical force P_cr = λ₁·N is unchanged to 3 × 10⁻⁷ (FR). The **stiffness-based stability limit is mesh-converged to about 10⁻⁶**. The remaining 10⁻⁵-level scatter in λ₁ reflects how finely the restrained thermal force is integrated.

**Classification: A (clearly convergent).**

- The smallest competing quantified uncertainty of λ₁ is the CFD-mesh effect, −0.5 % on one side (8A).
- 10 % of that is 5 × 10⁻⁴.
- The changes the rule uses are C → B 1.2 × 10⁻⁵ and fine vs B ≤ 6 × 10⁻⁶. They are **40 to 80 times below the threshold**.
- Even the extra-coarse XC differs from B by only 5.4 × 10⁻⁵, about one ninth of the threshold.

## 6. Buckling uncertainty: structural mesh vs the other sources

No combined total is formed. The sources differ in kind: some are bounds, some are sensitivity ranges and some are unquantified.

| Source | Effect on λ₁ | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| **End supports** (real flange restraint undefined, T-034) | λ₁ = 1.108 with sway free (LC2) vs **4.300 with the ends held laterally** (LC2NS), a factor of 3.9. An imperfect guided end (K ≈ 1.2) would give about 0.78 (−30 %) by Euler | 8A FE (LC2NS) and 8A hand estimate (AISC K values, information only) |
| **Temperature basis / CFD model** | Section 2 temperature basis: λ ≈ 0.98 (−11 %). The CFD model uncertainty (h/Nu correlation, 19 K on T_max; F-034) acts through the mean temperature and is **not quantified** as a λ change | 8A §7; 6B |
| **Material data** | the choice of E basis moves the hand λ by up to about ±2 % (E spread over the field). Datasheet scatter of E(T) and α(T) is not quantified. ν [ASSUMED] enters only through the shear term, which is 1.4 % of λ in total, so its effect is a fraction of that. No inelastic data (F-045): the Johnson estimate, 1.06, lies below the elastic value | 8A hand calculation |
| **CFD mesh** (type A) | +1.8 / −0.5 % | 6B mean-temperature band, via N (8A) |
| **Mapping** (type B) | about −0.02 % (volume mean conserved to +0.037 K) | 7A |
| **Structural mesh** (type C, this study) | ≤ 6 × 10⁻⁶ (fine vs B); ≤ 5.4 × 10⁻⁵ over the whole family; P_cr ≤ 2 × 10⁻⁶ | §5 |
| Imperfections, residual stress, plasticity, post-buckling | not analysed. Each lowers the real instability load below the linear eigenvalue | 8A §8 (F-044) |

**Ranking.** Supports ≫ temperature basis ≫ CFD mesh ≈ material (both of order 1 %) ≫ mapping ≫ structural mesh.

**What this means.** The structural mesh is the **smallest** contributor: about 1,000 times below the CFD-mesh and material effects, and further still below the support effect. So the value of λ₁ is not a mesh question. Refining the mesh cannot change the stability conclusion; defining the end restraint can.

## 7. How to read λ₁ = 1.108

The eigenvalue is the load multiplier at which the **ideal model** has a bifurcation. The model is:

- a perfectly straight duct of nominal geometry;
- on the idealised LC2 supports (end faces axially fixed, free to sway);
- with no imperfections or residual stress;
- in linear-elastic material, with no plasticity (σ_E ≈ 650 MPa is above the conventional proportional limit);
- with no post-buckling analysis.

**It is therefore a stability indicator, not a guaranteed real-world collapse load.** For a real duct the linear value is generally an upper estimate.

Because the LC2 load is a restrained thermal expansion (displacement-controlled), exceeding the critical state would appear as progressive lateral bowing that relieves part of the axial force. It would not appear as a sudden collapse under a sustained external load. The bowing would add bending stress to the 582 MPa compression.

The mesh study confirms that the **number** 1.108 is numerically robust. It does not change what that number represents.
