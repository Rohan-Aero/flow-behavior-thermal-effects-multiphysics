## 3. The Student limit and the design of the mesh family

The node count of this swept quadratic-hex mesh is exact:

- nodes = nc [(nr + 1)(na + 1)·2 + nr(na + 1) + (nr + 1)na];
- nc = circumferential, nr = through-wall, na = axial divisions.

The through-wall count is an integer, so the smallest possible refinement in that direction is 5 → 6 (×1.2).

| Candidate fine mesh | nc × nr × na | Nodes | Fits 128,000? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Uniform r = 1.30 (the family ratio) | 48 × 7 × 169 | 252,576 | no (×2.0) |
| Uniform r ≈ 1.2 | 42 × 6 × 156 | 177,744 | no |
| Smallest refinement touching all three directions | 39 × 6 × 143 | 151,359 | no |
| Through-wall + circumferential | 39 × 6 × 130 | 137,670 | no |
| Through-wall + axial | 36 × 6 × 140 | 136,800 | no |
| **FR**: through-wall only | 36 × 6 × 130 | **127,080** | yes (99.3 %) |
| **FA**: axial only | 36 × 5 × 152 | **126,468** | yes (98.8 %) |
| **FC**: circumferential only | 42 × 5 × 130 | **126,294** | yes (98.7 %) |

**The strongest defensible alternative, used here.** A true FINE level of the family cannot be built, so the study combines three parts. Each is labelled for what it is.

1. **Systematic family XC → C → B (COARSE side).**
   - Every direction is scaled by the same ratio: element count 4,788 / 10,584 / 23,400, so r = (23,400 / 10,584)^(1/3) = 1.303 in element size.
   - The bias factor 4 and the topology are kept.
   - This gives a three-level Richardson / GCI estimate with the **baseline as the finest level** (Celik et al. 2008). This is the standard route when the finest affordable mesh is the one in use.
   - XC is an extra coarse level. It exists only so that the three-level procedure is possible.
2. **Licence-limited directional FINE meshes FR, FA, FC.** Each refines one direction of the baseline as far as the licence allows, while the other two are held.
   - Together they show which direction still matters, and by how much, at the finest resolution the licence permits.
   - They are **not** a uniform fine level and are not used in the Richardson formula.
3. **Local inlet check IL** (a separate sensitivity check, not part of the family).
   - The same element count as B, with the axial bias raised from 4 to 8: first element 1.36 mm instead of 2.13 mm, at the price of 10.9 mm instead of 8.5 mm at mid-span.
   - IL is used only to see whether the inlet peaks depend on local resolution. It is never used as the refinement strategy.

**Mapping to the brief's COARSE / BASELINE / FINE.**

- COARSE = C (27 × 4 × 98), plus XC.
- BASELINE = B (36 × 5 × 130).
- FINE = the licence-limited set FR / FA / FC.

## 4. Mesh definition and quality

{{T1}}

**Quality checks and their findings.**

- **Element type.** All elements are quadratic hexahedra (SOLID186, 20 nodes).
- **Swept-mesh shape metrics.** Skewness ≤ 0.095 and warping ≤ 2 × 10⁻¹³ on every mesh. These are Mechanical metrics.
- **No invalid or inverted elements.** Checked independently of Mechanical by `Post/mesh_geometry_8B.py`, which reads each solver input deck:
  - every element has 20 distinct nodes;
  - the corner Jacobian (edge triple product) is positive at all 8 corners of every element, 0 failures.
- **Connectivity.**
  - There are no coincident or unreferenced nodes.
  - Every internal face is shared by exactly two elements.
  - The number of free faces equals 2·nc·na + 2·nc·nr exactly, so there are no cracks and no duplicated surfaces.
- **Aspect ratio.**
  - The largest aspect ratio (4.2–6.3) is set by the long mid-span elements; the bias puts the finest elements at the two ends.
  - These values are far below the gate limit of 20.
  - IL, with bias 8, has the highest aspect ratio (6.26) and the lowest element quality (0.15), as expected.

## 5. Temperature mapping on every mesh

The same source node field is mapped on each mesh. The check `Post/mapping_check_8B.py` uses the 7A validation modules unchanged and compares Mechanical's BFBLOCK with two references:

- **(A)** the exact shape-function value of the Fluent solid node field at each structural node;
- **(B)** the Fluent finite-volume solution.

{{T2}}

**Findings.**

- **Coverage.** 0 unmapped nodes on every mesh. The range is identical to 0.003 K (423.837–562.560 K).
- **Extrapolation.**
  - The largest value exceeds the source-node maximum by only ≤ 0.016 K.
  - This happens at outer-surface "chord-gap" nodes, which lie on the true circle just outside the source 48-gon.
  - It is a projection effect of that size, not an extrapolation outside the source range in any meaningful sense.
- **Mapping error proper, (A).** ≤ 0.09 K inside the source mesh and ≤ 0.14 K on chord-gap nodes, on every mesh.
- **Transfer difference, (B).** 2.3–2.6 K in the first 14 mm (the known F-035 zone) and ≤ 0.28 K elsewhere. It is **the same on every mesh**: it belongs to the source field, not to the structural mesh.

## 6. LC1 (free expansion, thermal only)

{{T3}}

**Deformation is converged.**

- Maximum total deformation and free growth change by ≤ 3.3 × 10⁻⁵ over all meshes (0.06 µm).
- Monotonic convergence, with a Richardson estimate within 2 × 10⁻⁶ of the baseline.

**Mid-span bore stress: a small, known under-prediction.** The finer the through-wall resolution, the higher the bore hoop stress:

- XC 17.998 MPa (3 through-wall elements);
- C 18.213 (4);
- B 18.309 (5);
- FR 18.425 (6).

Axial and circumferential refinement change it by ≤ 0.13 %, so the through-wall direction is what matters. An exact reference was built for this location: `Post/ref1d_lc1_8B.py`.

- **How it works.** It solves the axisymmetric generalised-plane-strain thick-cylinder problem with 2,000 elements, using:
  - the source temperature at z = 300 mm, evaluated directly from the Fluent node field and θ-averaged;
  - E(T), the MPAMOD thermal strain and ν exactly as in the solver input.
- **Solver check.** Against the closed-form Timoshenko solution (constant E and α, logarithmic profile), the 1-D solver agrees to 0.06 %.
- **Result.** Bore σθ = **18.698 MPa**.
- **Comparison with the FE.**
  - B is **2.08 %** low (0.39 MPa).
  - FR is 1.46 % low.
  - The Richardson estimate of the family (18.387 MPa) is also 1.7 % low. The family does not isolate the through-wall direction, so its extrapolation under-shoots.
- **Where the error comes from.** `Post/ref1d_split_8B.py` repeats the 1-D solution with each mesh's own nodal temperatures, interpolated quadratically across its through-wall elements exactly as SOLID186 does.
  - The result is 18.69–18.71 MPa on every mesh, within 0.1 % of the exact profile.
  - **The temperature representation is therefore not the cause.** The 2 % is the recovery of the surface stress (Gauss-point extrapolation plus nodal averaging) across a steep through-wall stress gradient on 2 mm elements.

**Inlet peak (bore, z ≈ 6.5–8.7 mm): non-monotonic in the family, and all refinements move it up by about 1 %.**

- The coarser family levels change it by −0.5 % (XC) and −0.9 % (C).
- The fine variants and IL change it by +1.10 % (FR), +1.11 % (FA), −0.32 % (FC) and +0.94 % (IL).
- **Why it is non-monotonic.** The peak lies between node planes, and each mesh samples it at a different z. The through-wall part of the recovery error is as at mid-span.
- If the FR and FA effects are roughly additive, the converged peak is about **24.5–24.9 MPa**, against 24.28 MPa on B. This is an estimate, not a solved value.
- It is still far inside the F-035 temperature-transfer bound of ±9.8 MPa at this location.

**Reactions.** |ΣF| ≤ 7.6 × 10⁻⁷ N on every mesh: the rigid-body supports stay reaction-free.

## 7. LC2 (axially restrained, thermal only)

{{T4}}

**Converged on the baseline.** Relative to B, the finest meshes change the governing values as follows.

| Quantity | Largest change | Absolute |
|---|---|---|
| Peak von Mises | FR +0.0098 % | 0.06 MPa |
| Mean axial stress N/A | FC +7.3 × 10⁻⁶ | 0.004 MPa |
| End reaction | same as mean stress | ≤ 4.1 N of 548,937 N |
| Max total, axial and radial deformation | ≤ 2.1 × 10⁻⁵ | 3 nm |

**Critical location.** It is the same on every mesh: the outer edge of the inlet face (r = 20 mm, z = 0) at 437.98–437.99 K.

- Its circumferential position differs between meshes (θ = −170° on B, −94° on XC).
- This has no meaning: the stress around that edge is uniform to 0.003–0.025 MPa (the maximum minus the θ-mean), so the discrete maximum can land anywhere on the ring.

**The interior maximum (15–585 mm) oscillates within 0.4 %.**

- It is a flat maximum sampled between node planes (591.2 MPa on B).
- The fine variants agree with B within 0.05 %.

**The end reaction moves by up to 7 × 10⁻⁶ with through-wall and circumferential refinement.** That reflects how the section integral of the thermal strain is represented. It is already 1 × 10⁻⁵ from the analytical restrained force (7B).

## 9. Convergence assessment

### 9.1 Classification rule and why this threshold

**Why a fixed percentage was not used.** A fixed limit such as "< 1 %" would be arbitrary. Instead, each quantity's structural-mesh sensitivity is compared with the **smallest competing uncertainty already quantified for that same quantity**, usually the CFD-mesh effect of Section 6B carried through 7B/8A. A structural-mesh error matters only if it can change the uncertainty band that is already reported.

| Class | Rule |
|---|---|
| **A** clearly convergent | Every structural-mesh change (C → B, and each licence-limited fine mesh vs B), and GCI_fine where defined, is **≤ 10 %** of the smallest competing uncertainty. The family also converges monotonically, or the changes are at numerical-noise level (≤ 10⁻⁴), where a Richardson ratio has no meaning. **Why 10 %:** even if added linearly, the mesh error then widens the reported band by at most one tenth |
| **B** approximately convergent | The changes are below the competing uncertainty but above 10 % of it, or they oscillate within a bounded band below it |
| **C** mesh-sensitive | The structural-mesh change, or a demonstrated bias, is **comparable to or larger than** the competing uncertainty |
| **D** inconclusive | The data cannot establish a trend or bound |

| Mesh | XC | C | B | FR | FA | FC | IL |

| LC1 unaveraged / averaged − 1 | +0.83 % | +0.84 % | +0.30 % | +0.29 % | +0.97 % | +0.19 % | +0.79 % |
| LC2 unaveraged / averaged − 1 | 9 × 10⁻⁷ | 7 × 10⁻⁸ | 4.0 × 10⁻⁵ | 3.7 × 10⁻⁵ | 4.1 × 10⁻⁵ | 1.1 × 10⁻⁵ | 4.1 × 10⁻⁵ |
| LC1 energy-norm error √(e/(U+e)) | 1.84 % | 0.97 % | 0.70 % | 0.56 % | 0.72 % | 0.61 % | 0.70 % |
| LC2 energy-norm error | 0.031 % | 0.017 % | 0.012 % | 0.009 % | 0.012 % | 0.010 % | 0.012 % |

(U is the strain energy of the 7B baseline solution, 0.154 J for LC1 and 505.6 J for LC2.)

**Reading the table.**

- **The raw-vs-averaged gap stays small.** It is ≤ 1 % for LC1 and ≤ 4 × 10⁻⁵ for LC2 on every mesh, and it does not grow with refinement. A stress singularity would make it grow.
- **The energy-norm estimate falls monotonically along the family** (apparent order p ≈ 4.4).
- **Only through-wall and circumferential refinement lower it further** (FR −20 %, FC −12 %). Axial refinement (FA) and the inlet bias (IL) do not.
- **Conclusion:** the remaining discretisation error sits in the through-wall stress gradient, which agrees with §6.

## 11. Inlet region (first ~15 mm)

Figure F8B_09 shows the θ-mean profiles of all seven meshes.

**LC2, outer surface.**

- On every mesh the profile falls from 605.1–605.2 MPa at the face to about 587 MPa at 30 mm.
- The face value changes by ≤ 0.01 % with through-wall, axial, circumferential or local (IL, first element 1.36 mm) refinement.
- **It is not a singularity.**
  - The U_z = 0 face support with free radial sliding acts as a symmetry plane: there is no re-entrant corner and no point constraint.
  - Refining the edge does not raise the value.
  - The unaveraged-vs-averaged gap is 4 × 10⁻⁵.
- The peak is a real thermal-stress feature: the cold, entrance-region bore temperature plus full axial restraint.

**LC1, bore.**

- The end-effect peak at z ≈ 7 mm is resolved on every mesh from C upwards. XC is visibly too coarse: 3.6 mm first element, 3 through-wall elements.
- Refinement raises it by about 1 %: through-wall FR +1.1 %, axial FA +1.1 %, local IL +0.9 %.
- The circumferential direction does not raise it (FC −0.3 %).
- The rise is bounded and the same size as the mid-span through-wall effect. **It is not called a singularity.**
- The dominant uncertainty at this location remains the temperature transfer (F-035/F-040: ±9.8 MPa bound), not the mesh.

## 13. Mesh recommendation

**Keep the baseline mesh B (36 × 5 × 130, bias 4; 108,252 nodes, 23,400 SOLID186) for all further structural work.** The finest mesh is deliberately not chosen, for these reasons.

1. **The governing results are already converged on B.**
   - LC2 stresses, LC2 deformation, the restrained force and λ₁ / P_cr are class A.
   - No licence-limited fine mesh changes any of them by more than 1 × 10⁻⁴.
   - A finer mesh would buy nothing for the yield utilisation (0.578) or the stability factor (1.108).
2. **The fine meshes each improve only one direction, and use up the licence.**
   - FR (99.3 % of the limit) is the only variant that improves the one mesh-sensitive quantity, the LC1 bore stress: −2.1 % → −1.5 %.
   - LC1 stresses are about 18–25 MPa (utilisation 0.02) and drive no decision.
   - FR would leave no node budget for later model additions, for example flange or support stiffness for T-034 or imperfection models for T-035.
3. **Continuity.** B reproduces the official 7B/8A solutions bit for bit. Every reported number stays traceable to one model.
4. **What the known LC1 bias means.** If an LC1 surface stress ever becomes decision-relevant, quote it as **+2 % above the baseline value** (mid-span bore 18.7 MPa rather than 18.3 MPa), or use the through-wall-refined FR mesh for that single result.
